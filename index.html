Home My PhD Outside the lab Why a PhD?

I study awareness of unawareness in human and machine reasoning, with a focus on recognition of insufficiency: how a reasoner recognizes that the information available may not be enough for the task, even before it can identify what is missing.

I am interested in whether this recognition can be distinguished from uncertainty, what cues support it during ongoing reasoning, and how it develops into a control signal for sequential decisions about whether to continue searching or act.

I build controlled, matched tasks that go beyond final accuracy to compare how humans and language models monitor and regulate their reasoning.




Clusterwords

Clusterwords is a controlled semantic reasoning task based on Connections, designed for species-fair comparison of human and machine reasoning. Humans and language models receive matched problems and controlled word meanings, allowing us to manipulate information sufficiency and answer uncertainty separately.

Gentags: Discrete Semantic State for Constraint-Sensitive Decision Pipelines 2025 – 2026

First-author preprint introducing Gentags, a representation that compresses source text into short, evidence-grounded semantic units used as inspectable intermediate state in LLM decision pipelines. In a controlled study isolating representation structure, Gentags raised agreement with full-evidence decisions to 79.5% (vs. 52.3–61.6% for RAKE/YAKE/TF-IDF) and hard-constraint satisfaction to 97.3% (vs. 84.7–89.3%).
