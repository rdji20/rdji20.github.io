Awareness of unawareness

Uncertainty: exploring the current hypotheses inside your hypothesis set.

ℋt = {h₁, h₂, h₃}

Unawareness: the feeling that the correct hypothesis h* is not in the set at all.

P(h* ∉ ℋt) > 0

What does the agent do next?

We frame this not as search but as an information need: keep seeking information vs commit.

What alternative explanations matter?

Anything else that could cause the same information-seeking behavior without awareness of unawareness: ordinary uncertainty, low confidence, noise, missing evidence, misunderstanding, random exploration, a generic "always reconsider" strategy, search-cost differences.

Sequential decision-making

A decision problem is sequential when the agent does not choose only once; the agent faces later decision points whose circumstances may depend on earlier choices, information, or changes in preference.

bt action⟶ new evidence update⟶ bt+1 ⋯ commit⟶ final choice after the end⇢ learning

The final choice may or may not prompt action. The learning that happens after the end is outside our span (for now).

Bounded agent

A bounded agent cannot consider every possible explanation, action, or future consequence due to limited resources.

What is h? tentative

Currently working on this (August 8, 2026)

To determine what H is (the hypothesis space), we first need to determine what a single h is. Right now we have two models.

Model 1: stricter hypothesis boundary (Dougherty)

The intuition is a precursor. An h only exists once there is an explanatory candidate:

coherence signal generates⟶ h = golf terms

"These four feel connected" is not yet h.

Model 2: partial hypotheses (our proposal)

Once the person can deliberately represent "these four share some unknown relation", that already counts as a hypothesis, even though part of its content remains unspecified:

h = these four share r,  r = ?

Then later the content gets filled in:

r = ? ⟶ r = golf-related ⟶ r = golf scoring

While reading, extract answers to these questions

  • What is the representational object? Is it a rule, causal model, gist, category, explanation, structured relation?
  • Can it be incomplete or coarse? Can the person have "something about these belongs together" without the full explanation?
  • Can that partial representation guide a deliberate decision?
  • Can it later be refined while preserving earlier content?
  • When does the literature treat it as the same representation becoming more specific versus a new hypothesis replacing the old one?
  • What behavioral evidence shows that the representation is actually available to the person?

The Clusterwords example

Clusterwords (the game on the home page) is a sequential decision-making environment: at every guess you either commit a group or keep seeking. Suppose the board hides EAGLE, BIRDIE, BOGEY, PAR. The diagnostic sequence is:

"these feel related" ⟶ "these four belong together somehow" ⟶ "golf-related" ⟶ "golf scoring terms"

We are trying to determine where h begins. Is it here?

h = "these four belong together somehow"

Or only here?

h = "golf-related"

Or only with the fully explanatory:

h = "golf scoring terms"

The reason this matters is still the same: until we know that boundary, we cannot distinguish an underspecified hypothesis from a missing hypothesis, which is necessary for defining awareness of unawareness.
